The Company

Both Sides combines a distinctive consumer news app with an in-house AI editorial newsroom harness. We have built the publishing engine and published our apps on Google Play and the Apple App Store. We are now preparing to bring the product to a wider audience through creator partnerships.

Our product helps readers understand a news event and the competing arguments around it in one card. Our technology coordinates the work behind that card: collecting coverage, organizing topics, filtering sources, drafting the story, checking supporting quotations, selecting an image and publishing to the apps.

The newsroom harness is our primary competitive edge. It brings editorial production and operational controls together in one system, with a current AI-and-server bill of INR 1,02,500 per month and publishing output of more than 50 cards a day.

Our ambition is to make Both Sides India's No. 1 news startup for readers who want to understand competing perspectives. We are building toward world-class standards in product experience, editorial workflow, engineering and cost efficiency.

Our Edge: The AI Editorial Newsroom Harness

We have built the system that turns incoming news coverage into a finished Both Sides card. The harness coordinates specialized AI work with deterministic code, source checks and publishing controls.

This combination lets us automate repetitive production work while keeping clear rules around what moves through the pipeline. Each part supports the same objective: produce a readable account of the event and its competing positions through an efficient, repeatable workflow.

The harness performs nine connected activities:

  1. COLLECT COVERAGE Monitor 76 Indian outlets and gather additional coverage through the major news search indexes and our own recent-story index.
  1. ORGANIZE TOPICS Use our own code to group related headlines and identify coverage of the same event.
  1. SELECT STORIES Apply timing, freshness and duplication rules to determine which topics advance through the publishing workflow.
  1. FILTER SOURCE MATERIAL Use relevance filtering to identify articles about the selected story and reduce unrelated material before it reaches the AI stages.
  1. DRAFT THE CARD Coordinate AI writing of the event account, the central claims and the explanation of each position.
  1. CHECK AGAINST SOURCES Run an AI checking stage against the collected articles, followed by code that checks supporting quotations against source text.
  1. CONTROL STORY VERSIONS Associate checks with the corresponding story version so publishing uses the text that passed through the checking workflow.
  1. PREPARE THE IMAGE Gather candidate photographs, select a suitable image, crop it to the card format and preserve its credit. Labeled illustrations provide a fallback.
  1. PUBLISH AND MONITOR Deliver the completed card to the apps through Firebase, with publishing gates, status monitoring, alerts and recovery procedures.

Our principal AI stages cover writing, checking and picture selection. Code handles collection, organization, filtering, workflow state and many of the checks surrounding those AI stages. This design concentrates model use on the work that calls for it.

Engineering Already In Place

The operating system includes: - A single always-on application on a cloud server. - A continuous engine that advances work around the clock. - Ten AI workers for job execution. - A topic sorter calibrated on 6,300 real headlines. - Code-based relevance filtering. - Source-quotation matching and story-version fingerprints. - Publishing gates for required content and workflow states. - Automated image retrieval, cropping and credit handling. - Continuous health checks. - Automatic recovery sweeps. - Status reporting, commands and alerts on the founder's phone. - Verified nightly backups. - Controlled restarts with an operator decision before publishing resumes. - Six test suites and documented deployment procedures.

The routine publishing workflow runs without a continuously staffed production desk. The founder retains operational control through status checks, alerts and pause/resume commands.

This is a substantial part of the company's execution story: the backend, publishing process and mobile distribution are already in place as we enter the audience-building phase.

The Reader Experience

Every Both Sides card brings together

WHAT HAPPENED A concise account of the event gives the reader a starting point.

THE COMPETING CLAIMS Each side's central position appears in a short, accessible form.

THE ARGUMENTS BEHIND THEM The reader can explore how the sides explain their positions and reach their own conclusion.

This format makes comparison part of the reading experience. It reduces the effort of moving between separate creators, articles and editorial voices to understand the disagreement.

The product is free. AI-written content is identified as such. Our publishing workflow checks supporting quotations against source text, while the reader retains the freedom to form a judgment.

The Audience We Are Building For

Both Sides is built for younger Indian readers who encounter public affairs through social feeds and want to understand the competing arguments.

We focus on Gen Z and millennials who value an accessible explanation and the freedom to decide what they think. A reader can hold a strong opinion and still want to understand another position.

Our ambition is to make that curiosity a direct app habit. A clear card format gives us a consistent experience to demonstrate through creators and develop with reader feedback.

Distribution: Creators Bring The Product To Readers

Our initial acquisition strategy centers on YouTube and Instagram creators. The demonstration is built around an actual story:

  • A creator introduces the event.
  • They explain the competing cases in their own voice.
  • They show how Both Sides presents the story in the app.
  • Interested viewers can install the app and continue reading.

This approach puts the product in front of people already engaging with news and public affairs. It gives prospective readers a concrete reason to try Both Sides.

The immediate funding objective is to launch this acquisition effort and build a returning audience. The six-month audience goal in our pitch is 100,000 daily app users.

Free Access And Commercial Opportunity

Both Sides will remain free for readers. Consumer subscriptions are outside our commercial strategy.

As the audience grows, we plan to explore advertising as an operating revenue opportunity. Our first priority is developing the reader experience and a regular audience. Advertising is a future commercial option that can be developed alongside that audience.

Our intended exit is a strategic acquisition by a traditional publisher seeking stronger engagement with younger readers.

We are building toward a combination of assets that can matter to such a publisher:

A RECOGNIZABLE CONSUMER BRAND Both Sides has a clear promise and a distinctive way of presenting news.

A DIRECT READER RELATIONSHIP Our audience-building strategy seeks to establish repeat use inside our own Android and iOS apps.

AN AI EDITORIAL NEWSROOM HARNESS The publishing technology supports the product and provides an additional reason for a publisher to evaluate the company.

A LEAN OPERATING FOUNDATION The automated workflow and defined direct cost base create a starting point for a publisher to assess the operating opportunity.

Our acquisition strategy brings the audience opportunity and the technology opportunity together. We intend to build a company whose product, brand and publishing capability are valuable to own.

Pitch Deck: All 20 Slides

Slide-by-slide text of the September 2026 Both Sides pitch deck, in our own words. The deck itself is an animated web page; this is its full content.

01Both Sides

One story. Both sides. Your judgment.

Both Sides is a news app for India. Social media shows each person one side. We show everyone both sides.

02The App

Every story is one card. Both sides on it.

  • At the top, what happened, in one line nobody disputes.
  • Then each side's claim, in a sentence, side by side.
  • Then each side's full case, in its own words. We never pick a winner.

Free, no ads, no trackers. Written by AI, and says so. Fact-checked before it publishes. On Google Play and the App Store. The slide shows a screenshot from the Android app, 8 September 2026.

03The Market Today

News comes from two kinds of places: news apps, built for speed, and traditional outlets, built around their own reporting. Both kinds tell each story in one voice.

04Where India Reads

News already lives inside the apps people open every day.

Among India's online news users, over half use YouTube for news each week and more than a third use Instagram (Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025, India; mainly English-speaking online news users).

The habit exists. We fit into it rather than ask people to build a new one. Source: https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/india

05Two Jobs

News has two jobs. Both Sides does the second one.

Someone finds what happened: reporters, and agencies like ANI. Then someone tells it: television, newspapers, creators, and now Both Sides. The race is for the second job: which teller people choose.

06A Habit Per Generation

The format changes. The need does not.

Our grandparents read the paper every morning. Our parents watched the nine o'clock bulletin. Younger people follow creators on YouTube and Instagram, in the feed, between everything else. People follow news in the format they already use.

07The Bubble

Social feeds split people into bubbles.

You follow the creators you like and avoid the ones you do not, and the feed learns from every click. Soon each side feels it is seeing the whole world when it sees only one part.

Both Sides puts both bubbles in one place. No creator to like or dislike. Just both arguments.

08One Story, Two Cases

One story. Two strong cases. You decide.

We start with what happened, make the strongest case for each side, and stop. About ten minutes a day, and no verdict from us. The reader gets enough to understand the disagreement; the judgment stays theirs.

09Our First Audience

Gen Z and millennials who will not be told how to feel.

They see politics and public issues in their feeds all day and do not want to join a team. Not the left, not the right: the curious middle, which we believe is the biggest group. Their request is simple: do not spam me, do not tell me how to feel, show me both cases and I will decide. That is our starting belief. Retention will prove it.

10The News-App Habit

Indian news apps already reach millions every month, each on its own published measure:

  • Dailyhunt: over 60 million people a month.
  • Way2News: over 30 million monthly active users.
  • Dainik Bhaskar: 18 million unique mobile-app visitors (May 2025).
  • Inshorts: around 6.5 million monthly active users, company figure as reported.

We do not need to invent the habit. We need to win a small share of it with a clearer product.

11The Newsroom Today

How other news outlets and news apps work today.

News comes in from agencies and reporters. A person watches the feeds all day. An editor picks what will get clicks. A writer writes it in the outlet's voice, from one angle. A sub-editor tightens it and writes a grabby headline. It publishes: site, app, TV, push notification. The reader gets one voice, one angle.

Follow one story down the line and count the people at the desk: four. To run day and night, you need shifts of people, and the whole line leans toward whatever gets attention.

12What Newsrooms Spend

What news companies spend every month (company-wide operating expense, annual divided by 12, from public accounts):

  • Small digital outlets: between INR 60 lakh and over INR 2 crore a month.
  • Large outlets and apps: between INR 30 crore and INR 250 crore a month.
  • Both Sides: INR 1,02,500 a month, with nobody at the desk, publishing more than 50 cards a day.

On the chart the Both Sides bar is a hairline, so the slide draws it again at a hundred to one. The Both Sides figure excludes audience acquisition and future cloud costs as readers grow.

13What It Cost

Two numbers.

To build the phone app, once: INR 80,000 of AI subscriptions and INR 12,500 for the developer accounts on Google Play and the App Store. INR 92,500.

To run the newsroom, every month: two AI subscriptions, INR 1,00,000, and one remote server, INR 2,500. INR 1,02,500 a month, for more than 50 cards a day.

Nobody at the desk. The whole newsroom is two AI subscriptions and one remote server.

14How Many People

How many people it takes.

At other outlets: an office full of people, in shifts, day and night. Input desk and editors; writers and sub-editors; publishing to the site, the app and TV. On the payroll, company-wide, from 58 people at Newslaundry to 4,500 at DB Corp.

At Both Sides: one application, an AI editorial harness, placed on one remote server. It gathers, writes, checks and publishes, with code and AI. AI writes and checks; code does the rest. Zero people.

15The Machine, Sealed

What goes in, what comes out.

In: 76 Indian outlets, three global news indexes and free photo archives. Up to 800 articles gathered for every story.

Inside: nine stations, three AI rounds per story, eight publish gates, eight health checks and five healing sweeps. Three of the nine stations are the AI. The rest is code.

Out: more than 50 cards a day, the facts and then each side's case on every card, fact-checked before it publishes, with a picture, on Google Play and the App Store.

The machine is drawn in full in eight sheets, which are walked through on the first call.

16What It Took

The reader sees one card. It stands on nine layers of code and checks:

  • Three AI rounds per story, under strict contracts: a reply is accepted whole or rejected whole.
  • Nothing the AI wrote reaches a card unverified: every line is checked against its sources.
  • Eight publish gates, each one a hard stop: fail one and the story is parked, never pushed through.
  • An on-topic filter of our own, with no AI, tuned on 6,300 real headlines: it drops 85% of the pile and keeps 97% of the right articles.
  • 76 outlets watched round the clock, plus three global indexes; up to 800 articles gathered for every story.
  • An engine that never forces a story through: a story is published right, or not at all.
  • It checks itself round the clock: eight health checks, five healing sweeps, verified backups.
  • Locked down and tested end to end: 53 API routes, 14 screens, 125 configuration keys, six test suites.
  • A 5,662-line manual, just to describe what is there.

Everything under the water exists so the tip is right, every time, with nobody watching. The full drawings are shown on the first call.

17The Raise

We are raising money for two things.

  1. Influencer marketing. Creators bring the first readers: a creator takes one contested story, explains both cases in their own voice, and shows the app it came from. YouTube and Instagram first.
  2. An ANI subscription. Licensed, same-day news photographs on every story.

The product is built. The raise pays for distribution and images.

18Six Months Out

First, one lakh app readers. Then one show a day.

One lakh people using the app every day tells us the concept has merit. Other outlets meet people in many places and post all day; the readers we want hate that. We keep it to two channels. The app first. Then, once the app has one lakh users, one ten-minute YouTube run-down of the day's stories, without the sensation: two well-known hosts who stay neutral and two analysts, one for each side. It looks and feels like a podcast, not a newsroom. That is our no-spam policy, and it is the difference.
